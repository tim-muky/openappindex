# Funding policy

*Version 0.5 · decided 5 Oct 2026 · in force from 14 Oct 2026*

openAPPindex measures app stores and the apps in them. Whoever pays for that measurement could
want it to come out a certain way. This policy says what money can and cannot do here, and how
anyone can check.

The rule in one sentence: **no money buys a place in the index — and everyone who supports us
is named.**

---

## 1. What money can never buy

Nothing that appears in the index, the dataset or anything else we publish can be bought, by
anyone, at any price:

- whether an app is included, how it is described, where it appears or in what order;
- anything that works like placement: sponsored listings, "featured" spots, badges, advertising
  next to the index, affiliate links;
- a correction, a removal or a delay;
- advance notice of a finding, a preview, or a right to review before publication;
- a say in which categories we measure, how we classify apps, or what we sort by.

Anyone — supporter, customer, developer, stranger — can report an error to
korrektur@openappindex.org, and every report goes through the same public process. When a
correction was requested by someone who supports us, the dated correction notice says so.

## 2. Who may support us

Anyone may support openAPPindex on the terms of this policy — individuals, foundations, public
bodies and companies, **app publishers included**.

Why publishers are not excluded: app publishers as a group gain from an index that makes apps
findable by what they do and what they cost, rather than by who is already popular. Supporting
that is not buying a position in it, and section 1 makes sure it cannot become one. Excluding
every publisher would not even be possible: on 24 Sep 2026 the German App Store listed apps from
the EU's Publications Office, German federal agencies, Verbraucherzentrale NRW, Stiftung
Warentest, Mozilla and the Wikimedia Foundation (iTunes Search API, German storefront).

What we ask of it in return is disclosure, and three limits:

- **Support from an app publisher is unearmarked**, or tied to a work item already on our public
  roadmap. It cannot add, drop or prioritise a category, and it is never tied to an app.
- **Every app a supporter publishes is marked as such** on its page in the index:
  *"The provider of this app, or the organisation it belongs to, supports openAPPindex. Details:
  funding page."* A fact next to the other facts; it changes nothing about how the app is
  measured.
- **Anyone who asks for something from section 1** is turned down, whoever they are.

**The one exclusion: store operators.** We accept no money, in-kind support or anything else of
value from a company that operates an app store we measure — today **Apple and Google** — or from
any company it controls. They publish apps too, but they are also what the index measures: its
findings are about their stores. We look one step through: if Apple or Google is a named funder of
the specific programme, prize or fund that would pay us, we do not take that money.

**Concentration.** No single private supporter (a company, a foundation or an individual) provides
more than 25% of a year's income, so no supporter becomes one we cannot afford to lose. App
publishers, taken together, provide no more than 25% of a year's income. Public grants are
exempt from both caps.

## 3. The publisher who runs the index

openAPPindex is run by Tim Meyerdierks, who also builds and sells **galleybook**, a recipe app in
the cooking category we measure. The rules for that:

- galleybook is measured by the same published rules as every other app, and its page in the
  index states that it is built by the person who runs the index.
- galleybook gets no data that is not public: no unpublished measurements, no private runs, no
  early look at a snapshot. Any analysis for galleybook — including its app-store optimisation —
  runs only on the published dataset and published snapshots, the same files anyone can
  download.
- galleybook's store listing and marketing do not cite openAPPindex, and openAPPindex does not
  mention galleybook except on its app page and here.
- The index's costs are paid by Tim personally and disclosed as such (section 5). Any money from
  galleybook as a business would be listed like any other supporter.

## 4. What we accept, and how

- **Grants and public funding**, when the terms give the funder none of the rights in section 1.
  A funder may agree a work plan and milestones with us; it may not review, approve, delay or
  keep confidential what we find. We do not sign agreements that would allow it.
- **Prizes and fellowships** count as support and follow the same rules.
- **Support from organisations** — every organisation is named, whatever the amount.
- **Donations from individuals** — anyone who gives €1,000 or more in a calendar year is named,
  with their consent, or the gift is returned.
- **Earned income: we sell results, never placement.** Services built on the index — bulk data,
  alerts, reports, API access — are sold on a public price list, on the same terms for everyone,
  app publishers included. The facts stay open under CC BY 4.0; what is sold is convenience,
  format or timeliness, never exclusive facts and never influence. Every organisation that buys
  is named.
- **In-kind support counts as support.** Credits, sponsored plans or services given to us on
  application are listed at their stated value. Free plans that anyone can use on the same terms
  are not support; we list the ones we depend on.

## 5. What we publish

A funding page lists every supporter: name, amount, period, purpose, and the date agreed, added
within 30 days of an agreement. Once a year we publish totals by type of source — including the
**share that came from app publishers** — the share of the largest single source, and the amount
Tim contributed personally.

Grant applications are listed once decided, **awarded or declined**, with the date, the funder
and the amount requested.

## 6. Changing this policy

Changes are dated, versioned and published as a diff before they take effect. No change applies
retroactively. An offer that does not fit these rules is declined, or the rules are changed in
public first — there are no private exceptions.

When openAPPindex becomes a non-profit company, this policy moves into its articles of
association.
