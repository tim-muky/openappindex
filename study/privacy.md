# Privacy notice: "The cost of being found"

*A study of app publishers, above all new and small ones, by openAPPindex. Version 1.1 ·
30 October 2026 (version 1.0: 22 October 2026, the day the study opens; see section 7).*

This notice explains what personal data the study processes, why, on what legal basis, and what
rights you have. It follows Articles 13 and 14 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

tim@study.openappindex.org

openAPPindex is not legally required to appoint a data protection officer and has not appointed

## 2. What the study is

"The cost of being found" asks app publishers how people find their apps and what it costs to be
found. It has three parts:

1. an invitation email, and at most one reminder;
2. an online survey that does not ask who you are;
3. a separate, optional form for receiving the results and a snapshot of your app.

Taking part is voluntary. Not taking part has no consequences of any kind.

The results are published in aggregate in an open research paper (spring 2027). The study
protocol was published before the survey opened:
[openappindex.org/study/protocol.html](https://openappindex.org/study/protocol.html).

## 3. What we process, why, and on what basis

### 3.1 The invitation email and the reminder

- **Data:**
  - the contact email address you publish for your app;
  - your app's name, the developer name, the app's category, and its size group (worked out from
    the release date and the number of ratings).
- **Sources:**
  - the address: your own website, or your app's support or privacy page, where you publish it as
    a contact for the app. The invitation email names the exact page. On the day we write to
    you, we check that the address is still published there. Where you publish both a general
    inbox (such as support@ or info@) and a personal address on your own domain or website, we
    write to the personal one, because a general inbox often reaches a helpdesk rather than you.
  - The link to the example snapshot in the email shows only which email text it came from, so
    that openappindex.org can count how often the example is opened per text. It does not
    identify you, and the count holds no IP address (see openappindex.org/datenschutz.html).
  - everything else: your app's public App Store listing.
- **Purpose:** to invite you to take part, and to send one reminder about two weeks later. The
  survey is anonymous, so the reminder goes to everyone we invited, including people who have
  already answered. There is no newsletter and no other use.
- **Legal basis:** Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in independent research into how apps
  are discovered, published openly). We consider that interest to outweigh yours because:
  - the address is one you publish as a contact for your app;
  - we write at most twice, and never again after that;
  - nothing is sold, and the only thing offered is the study's own output (the paper, and a
    snapshot of your app's public App Store data);
  - you can object at any time with a one-word reply.
- **Storage:**
  - The address list is deleted when the survey closes on **31 January 2027**, or your address
    immediately if you reply "no".
  - So that nobody is invited twice, we keep a one-way fingerprint (a keyed hash from which the
    address cannot be recovered) of every address we wrote to, and of every address whose mail
    server rejected the email. These are deleted with the list on **31 January 2027**.
  - If you reply "no", we keep only the fingerprint of your address, so that you are not contacted
    again during the study. It is deleted on **31 January 2028**.

### 3.2 The survey

- **Your link doesn't identify you, and we won't try to identify you.** Your invitation link
  carries only:
  - your app's category and size group (e.g. "cooking, new and small");
  - the channel the invitation came through (e.g. the email or its reminder);
  - a random one-time number, used only to detect duplicate answers.

  We never store which address received which number. If you reply to an email, your reply may
  quote your link; we never use it to match answers (see 3.4).
- **What the survey asks:** it doesn't ask who you are. A name is asked only if you want to be
  quoted by name.
- **When your answers can become personal data:**
  - If you allow us to compare your answers with the public data we measured for your app, the
    survey then asks for your **App Store link**, and your answers relate to that app. If you
    publish the app as an individual, they relate to you.
  - The same applies if you choose to be **quoted by name**.
- **Legal basis:** your consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, given with the consent box at the start of the
  survey. Comparing your answers with our measurements needs a **separate** consent in the survey,
  and so does quoting your free-text answer.
- **Storage and use:**
  - Raw answers are seen only by the study lead.
  - For the analysis, the answers are processed with an AI service (see section 4) **after the
    name and App Store link fields have been removed**. Free-text answers are included, as
    written; please don't put personal details into them.
  - Raw answers are **deleted 12 months after the paper is published**, in the survey tool and in
    every export.
  - Aggregated results contain no individual answers and are published as open data under
    CC BY 4.0.
  - Quotes appear only as you allowed (anonymous by default).

### 3.3 The results form (optional)

- **Data:** your email address, your app category and, if you add it, your app's App Store link.
- **Purpose:** to send you the paper before publication and the results for your category, and,
  if you added the link, a one-page snapshot of how your app shows up in openAPPindex's
  measurements. The snapshot is made only from public App Store data and never from your survey
  answers. Snapshots are possible for apps in the two categories the index measured.
- **Legal basis:** your consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR.
- **Separation:** this is a separate form and is not connected to your survey answers. It is never
  shared with the AI service.
- **Storage:** deleted once the paper and results have been sent, and by **31 January 2028** at
  the latest.

### 3.4 If you reply to an email

- We use your reply only to answer you, or to record your "no".
- A "no" is recorded as a fingerprint (see 3.1) and the reply itself is deleted within 7 days.
- Other replies are kept until the study ends, and by 31 January 2028 at the latest.
- Automatic delivery-failure reports are deleted once the address is marked as undeliverable.
- Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (handling your message).

## 4. Who processes data on our behalf

We use these service providers:

- **Tally BV, Belgium.** Hosts the survey and the results form; form data is stored in the EU.
  Tally uses sub-processors, some of them outside the EU (for example in the USA), on the basis
  of the safeguards required by Art. 44 ff. GDPR (the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework or the EU
  standard contractual clauses), as described in Tally's documentation:
  [tally.so/help/gdpr](https://tally.so/help/gdpr). Tally processes technical data such as your
  IP address to operate the forms. We do not use that data for the study.
- **Heinlein Hosting GmbH (mailbox.org), Berlin, Germany.** Sends the invitation and reminder
  emails and receives replies, on servers in the EU.
- **Cloudflare, Inc., USA.** Delivers openappindex.org, including the study page and this notice,
  and processes the technical data needed for that, such as your IP address. Details and
  safeguards: [openappindex.org/datenschutz.html](https://openappindex.org/datenschutz.html),
  section 3.
- **Anthropic** (provider of the AI service Claude). Used for the analysis, on answers from which
  the name and App Store link fields have been removed. Data may be processed in the USA, on the basis of
  the safeguards in Anthropic's data processing terms.

Each provider is bound by a data processing agreement under Art. 28 GDPR. Nobody else receives
your data. It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to decide anything about
your app in openAPPindex. Taking part, or not, changes nothing about how your app appears in the
index.

## 5. Your rights

You have the right to:
- **access** your data (Art. 15);
- have it **corrected** (Art. 16) or **erased** (Art. 17);
- **restrict** its processing (Art. 18);
- **data portability** (Art. 20);
- **object** to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21): the invitation, the reminder,
  and the handling of your reply. Replying "no" is enough.

You may **withdraw your consent** at any time, with effect for the future (Art. 7(3)). Write to
tim@study.openappindex.org.

Because the survey does not ask who you are, we can find your answers only if you tell us your
App Store link or the approximate time you answered.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. Ours is:
**Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit**, Alt-Moabit 59–61,
10555 Berlin, [datenschutz-berlin.de](https://www.datenschutz-berlin.de).

## 6. No automated decisions

No decisions are made about you by automated means, and no profiles are created.
